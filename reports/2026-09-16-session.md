Nothing was quarantined for failing to parse. Every one of the 984 alerts

The parser fails closed: only an alert that yields all four of amount, card,
merchant and available limit reaches `success`. Everything else is quarantined
with its financial fields left `null`, so a quarantined row cannot be summed by
accident.

### The body's clock is never read

Card alerts print a 12-hour time with no meridiem — `04:19:02` is either 04:19
or 16:19, and the message contains nothing that decides which. `internalDate`
is therefore authoritative for time and the body timestamp is not parsed at
all.

### The matcher was wrong twice before it was right

Category (e) first read **84**, then **37**, then **12**. Both corrections were
found by spot-checking individual rows against the database, not by looking at
the count:

1. **One-directional prefix test.** `payeeKey` strips legal suffixes, so
   `BOOK MY FOREX PVT LTD` becomes `book my forex` — *shorter* than the SMS
   stump `book my forex p` it was supposed to match.
2. **Six-character minimum prefix.** `BUNDL TECHNOLOGIES` keys to `bundl`,
   five characters, because "technologies" is a stripped suffix.

Fixed by making the prefix test bidirectional, lowering the threshold to 4, and
stripping `UPI-<ref>-` before comparing.

### Merchant names: the finding that changed the recommendation

The working assumption going in was that email merchant names are a clean
superset of the SMS ones — the SMS truncates at 15 characters, the email does
not. On the matched rows (a+b+c, 907 emails):

| Comparison | Count | Share |
|---|---|---|
| Email string differs from stored payee | 620 | 68.4% |
| Differs ignoring case | 313 | 34.5% |
| Distinct `payeeKey` transitions, after stripping `UPI-<ref>-` | 70 | — |

The first two lines are the correction to what was reported yesterday. **620 /
68.4% was a case-sensitive comparison** — the emails are largely ALL CAPS and
the stored payees are proper-cased, so half the "difference" is casing that
`payeeKey` already folds away. The real divergence is 34.5%.

## 13. SMS parser: a tracking link read as a merchant name

Found while spot-checking the 12 unmatched alerts.

### The defect

```
INR 181.00 spent using ICICI Bank Card XX2003 on 14-Jun-26 on
https://icici.co/ICICIT/yoDJsE. Avl Limit: INR 75,456.71.
```

ICICI put a tracking link in the merchant slot. `cleanPayee()` took whatever
was there, so the link became the payee — and then a `merchant_categories` key,
and then a string handed to the model, which answered "Other" rather than
refusing.

The cache entry is what made this more than a display bug. A link is one-time,
so `https://icici.co/icicit/yodjse` was a key that could never be hit again,
sitting in the merchant list as though a URL were a place you can spend money.

### The fix

`cleanPayee()` returns `null` for a link, routing the row to review with the
link preserved in the note. Amount, card and date parse correctly and are still
trustworthy, so nothing is dropped — only the name that could not be learned is
left blank.

The rule is narrow on purpose, because real merchant names contain domains
constantly:

| Raw merchant | Parsed as | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| `https://icici.co/ICICIT/yoDJsE` | `null` | rejected |
| `AGODA.COM THE M` | `Agoda.com The M` | kept |
| `WWW.HOSTELWORLD` | `Www.hostelworld` | kept |
| `2CO.com*shop.mb` | `2CO.com*shop.mb` | kept |
| `Blinkit` | `Blinkit` | kept |

Only a scheme, or a domain followed by a path, marks a link. Anything keyed on
"contains a dot" or "starts with www" would eat three of those five. All five
are pinned in `src/lib/__tests__/classify.test.ts`.

### A bug in the repair script, caught before it mattered

The first run of `backfill_link_payees.mjs` printed `1000 transactions` for a
table holding 3,279. **PostgREST caps an unbounded select at 1000 rows and says
nothing about it**, so the script had checked less than a third of the table and
reported a clean bill of health for the rest. It pages explicitly now; the
"exactly 1 affected row" result above is from the full-table re-run.

  scripts as they now stand, and the conclusion it supported — that adopting
  email names is mostly a clean win — is **wrong** in either case. See the
  fragmentation analysis in §12.
